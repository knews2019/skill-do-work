3 Problem

io.Copy takes the side door

The launcher gives the buffers to os/exec as stdout and stderr. os/exec copies the pipe with io.Copy. io.Copy prefers ReadFrom when the writer has one. So bytes.Buffer.ReadFrom pulls in the whole stream, and our Write is skipped.

4 Problem

No limit and no error

A backend that prints too much fills memory without limit. The truncated flag stays false, so the error "backend output exceeded capture limit" can never fire. A copy of the old buffer kept 16,777,300 bytes with truncated=false.

5 Change

Give the buffer a name

The field became captured bytes.Buffer. A named field keeps its methods to itself, so the buffer has no ReadFrom. Every byte now goes through Write.

What this style is good and bad at

It is good when the story has a real chain of causes, like a bug or an incident. Each card answers why the next card happened, and every link carries its receipt.

It is weak for a small change with only one cause, where the chain becomes two thin cards. It also does not show how the parts connect in space. T2, the stepped diagram, does that better.
