Durations lane legibility — audit and alternatives

Kanban board · Durations view · Panel A overflow lane · 2026-08-18 · generated before implementation, for the maintainer to choose a direction

Verdict

The overflow lane is structurally out of room. REQ-226 fixed label-against-label collisions, and that fix is working — no two labels overprint each other in the submitted screenshot. What remains is the defect REQ-226 recorded but was scoped away from fixing: the mark band (y 35–45) and the first label row (text box roughly y 33–46) occupy the same vertical space, so on a dense board the dots overprint the text. At 51 overflow samples, roughly half of the few labels that placement can fit are then destroyed by neighbouring marks. Fixing it requires giving the lane more height — exactly the change REQ-226 was told not to make — so this is a small deliberate redesign, not a bug fix, and it is worth choosing the shape on purpose.

Root cause

The lane exists because y carries no magnitude above the 60-minute ceiling — every overflow mark sits at the constant DURATIONS_LANE_MARK_Y = 40, so text is the only carrier of the value. But the lane is 36 user-units tall and asks that height to hold a mark band and two text rows: row 0's baseline is y 44 while marks with radius 5 span y 35–45. The collision rule shipped in REQ-226 (durations.go · planDurationLabels) deliberately guards label-against-label only. Every alternative below therefore starts from the same move — text and marks stop sharing vertical space — and differs in what the lane's text is for.

Alternative 1 — separated text band (minimal fix)

The lane grows 14 user-units. Marks keep the top strip; both label rows move below a dashed divider, with a 1px leader tick tying each label to its mark. Selection logic is untouched — same greedy first-fit, same remainder.

Cost: smallest. Lane height + panel offsets in two files; no selection change.
Trade-off: labels are still an arbitrary first-fit sample (finding 3 stays).

Alternative 2 — top-N extremes in the text band (recommended)

Same band separation as Alternative 1, but the labels change meaning: only the N longest spans (six here) are labelled, in the band, with leader ticks. The remainder counts everything else. Every visible label is now a superlative — which is what a reader scanning an outlier lane actually wants — and the lane never again depends on packing luck.

Cost: moderate. Selection rule in durations.go changes from first-fit to top-N-by-magnitude (placement stays collision-aware); renderer band geometry; tests.
Trade-off: a mid-sized outlier the maintainer cares about may be unlabelled — but it always was; hover and the table still carry every sample.

Alternative 3 — log-scale overflow region

The flat lane becomes a compressed log scale (1h → 24h) above the break, so position carries magnitude again: a 16-hour span visibly towers over a 90-minute one, unlabelled marks still inform, and only the top three need text at all.

Cost: largest. New scale, new ticks, new hover math, ~30 extra units of height, and it re-opens the panel design REQ-226 declared settled.
Trade-off: most informative option; also the only one that changes what the panel is rather than how it labels.

Recommendation

Alternative 2. It is the smallest change that fixes both real findings: band separation closes the collision structurally (not just at today's density), and top-N selection makes the lane's only text answer the question the lane exists for — "where are the outliers". It keeps Panel A's scale-break design, the payload-carries-the-verdict architecture from REQ-219/226 (selection still computed in durations.go, shipped as labelRow/labelAnchor), the hover readout, and the table, all unchanged in kind. If more vertical budget is acceptable and the panel may be rethought, Alternative 3 is the more informative design — but it is a redesign, not a fix.

Aside, out of scope: adjacent route blues measure close together for normal vision under the dataviz validator (ΔE ≈ 13 between the two mid blues in the screenshot's palette). The legend, hover, and table already mitigate; worth a look only if the palette is ever revisited.
