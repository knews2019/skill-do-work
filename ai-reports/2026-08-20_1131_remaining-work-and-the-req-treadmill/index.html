Verdict

The queue is not a backlog. It is an equilibrium.

Throughput is not the constraint. Between 3 and 20 August the pipeline created 228 REQs and finished 197. The median REQ takes 20 minutes of wall time; the whole 31-REQ queue is roughly twelve to twenty hours of run time. It could be drained in two days. It has never drained because intake tracks output almost exactly, and it does so because the pipeline is its own largest customer: 145 of the 298 REQs ever written were spawned by a prior REQ's review or build, and in the current queue that share is 19 of 31. Only three of the 31 came from neither a prior REQ nor an audit batch.

The brake already exists and is not engaged. UR-060 built exactly the mechanism that would stop this — an impact: field plus a --skip-impact-negligible filter. Nineteen of the 31 pending REQs carry no impact: field at all, and an absent field reads as impact-user-visible. The filter currently removes one REQ from the queue. REQ-294 is the REQ that says why: three forces in the capture path push the value to user-visible, and the anti-invention guard blocks impact-negligible — the one value the filter needs.

Seven of the 31 should be closed with a sentence, not built. Four class-closing REQs retire generators with documented histories — REQ-269 alone ends a defect class that has produced eight consecutive REQs. Ten are genuine defects that must ship. The rest are prose reconciliation, and prose reconciliation is the engine.

The exit is a rule change, not a sprint. A discrepancy between two sentences in the instruction corpus should stop being a REQ. Make it an entry on one standing sweep that runs on a cadence. Everything else in this report is sequencing.

Queue depth is not drifting upward. It was held between zero and seven for the whole of early August, which means the pipeline can reach empty and repeatedly did. The current 31 is a step function, not a trend: two upstream consumer reports and one review round landed on 18–20 August against near-zero completions.

What the flat stretch proves

Between 1 and 17 August the queue sat at four or fewer on eleven of seventeen days. A pipeline that reaches zero that often is not overloaded. It is being refilled at the rate it empties.

Finding F3

The brake was built. It is disengaged.

This is not an unrecognized problem. skills/do-work/actions/review-work.md:343 states the failure mode in the repository's own words: "one UR's review chain minted sixteen REQs over two days, fifteen of them facets of a single root cause." UR-060 exists to fix it. The mechanism shipped: an impact: enum, a mandatory token on every review finding, sweep-REQ folding for shared root causes, and a --skip-impact-negligible run filter.

An absent field is read as impact-user-visible by design, so the filter treats 25 of 31 as must-do work.

Why the filter can't bite

REQ-294 names the mechanism directly: three separate forces in the shipped capture path push a new REQ's impact: to impact-user-visible, and the only stated anti-invention guard blocks the one value UR-060 exists to surface. The template pre-fills a verdict, and the guard that is supposed to stop invention only fires in the direction that would reduce the queue.

REQ-297 is the second half: --skip-impact-negligible has two reporting paths and a targeted run reaches neither, so a skip is invisible. A filter nobody can audit is a filter nobody trusts. Together these two REQs are the only work in the queue that changes the arrival rate rather than the queue length.

The exit

Three moves, then a rule change

Draining the queue is not the exit — the graph in F1 shows it has been drained repeatedly and refilled every time. These are ordered so that the rate changes before the length does.

MOVE 1

Close seven, unblock four

Tier E, in one sitting. REQ-300 and REQ-302 close on their own stated reasoning; REQ-262 is a decision; REQ-272 and REQ-273 are single edits. Closing REQ-300 releases the depends_on gates on REQ-263, REQ-264, REQ-271 and REQ-301 — the four-deep critical path named in the restart prompt.

Result: 31 → 24 pending, critical path collapses from four deep to flat. Cost: under an hour.

MOVE 2

Finish the brake before touching anything else

Tier A: REQ-294, REQ-297, REQ-293, then REQ-288 and REQ-269. Until impact: is judged rather than defaulted and the skip is auditable, every review round after this one adds work at the current rate, and the four class-closers in Tier B will each spawn their own review follow-ups on the way out.

Result: the next review round can produce a queue that shrinks. This is the only work here that changes the derivative.

MOVE 3

Retire the generators, then ship the defects

Tier B (REQ-298, REQ-299, REQ-291 → REQ-292) before Tier C, because each one removes a class that would otherwise keep producing. Then Tier C in the order listed — REQ-279 and REQ-303 first, since one cries data loss falsely and the other ships a failing test to every consumer install.

Result: queue reaches single digits with the arrival rate already lowered, so it stays there.

The rule change that actually ends it

The pipeline currently treats any discrepancy between two statements in the instruction corpus as a defect that earns a REQ. Across 156 cross-referencing markdown files under skills/, the number of possible discrepancies grows with the square of the corpus, and every fix writes new sentences that can drift. That is the generator. Reviews are just what finds it.

Four of the five deepest descent chains in this repository terminate in prose or comment reconciliation — REQ-250 and REQ-243 on markdown pointers, REQ-277 and REQ-266 on comment provenance — and the eight-deep chain in F2 turns from product work into corpus-consistency work at generation five. Both REQ-266 and REQ-277 are still pending, at generations six and seven, and both are Tier E hold-candidates here. The archive holds seventy-one maintenance: true REQs.

Proposal: a prose-only discrepancy — a stale count, a wrong cross-reference number, a comment that describes a superseded mechanism — stops being a REQ. It becomes a line appended to one standing sweep REQ that is run on a cadence you choose, or folded into the next commit that touches the file for another reason. Reserve REQ creation for changes to behavior, to a checker's predicate, or to a rule's stated condition. This is the same judgment review-work.md:349 already encodes for impact-negligible findings — applied to the class that is actually producing the volume, and applied at capture rather than at review.

Open questions

Three things this analysis cannot decide for you

Q1 · What cadence for the standing sweep?

Prose reconciliation still has to happen eventually — the Check 11/Check 12 error in REQ-272 does send readers to the wrong place. Weekly, per release, or per N merged REQs are all defensible. The point is that it batches, not that it stops.

Q2 · Do consumer reports get the same treatment?

Nine of the 31 arrived as upstream-consumer-report batches, and those are outside intent — a real user hit them. They may deserve exemption from the prose-sweep rule even when the finding is prose-shaped.

Q3 · Is the corpus itself too large?

Not analyzed here, and the more interesting question. The generator's rate scales with cross-references, not with features. Deleting instruction text would lower it more permanently than any checker will.
