Why the queue drains slowly, and what to change in the speed fix

Development velocity of the do-work queue, measured from the archive, the calibration log, the recorded gate runs and the queue on disk. Follow-up to the 00:10 gate-cost analysis and the 2026-09-01 queue analysis. Evidence mode: non-visual. UI captures were not expected for this work.

Verdict

The speed fix is the right fix and it is stalled. REQ-537 (tiering the gate into fast and heavy) shipped at 16:06 with no speedup: the fast tier measured 629 s against the heavy tier's 631 s because the board's test package, which is 73% of the gate, is still not skippable until REQ-538 lands. REQ-538 (cutting the board tests to a fast core) has been claimed since 19:49 local by a cloud session that is now idle, and a separate Codex worktree is doing the same cuts on its own branch without the claim. Nothing in the batch has moved for over two hours.

Three changes to the batch, in order: unblock REQ-540 from REQ-539 so the two Go-side cuts land first, add a mechanical 30-second-per-file budget that the gate measures and enforces, and rewrite REQ-541 so a REQ that touches a heavy surface goes to a pending-heavy-testing status instead of appending to a standing sweep. The maintainer said this afternoon that per-REQ status is the shape they want; that supersedes decision D4 in UR-104.

The gate is the largest single lever but not the only one. A REQ now takes a median of 65 minutes from claim to archive, and two gate runs cost about 21 of those minutes. The other structural cost is the queue's shape: 38 pending, 20 ready, and one serial chain of ten REQs (REQ-502 through REQ-510, then REQ-486) that no amount of parallel building shortens.

The unclaimed parallel work

A git worktree on branch codex/test-budget-heavy-consent holds an uncommitted diff of 34 insertions and 35 deletions in the board package. It deletes exactly the two RejectsZeroProbes meta-tests REQ-538 names, replaces them with a table-driven unit test of the guard, and adds QUEUE_KANBAN_JAVASCRIPT_PROBES and QUEUE_KANBAN_BROWSER_PROBES as off switches. That is REQ-538's first two requirements, done outside the claim. If the maintainer accepts it, the cheapest path is to fold it into REQ-538 and release the idle cloud claim; if not, the Codex branch should stop before it and the claimed session collide on the same files.

The same session measured the wider corpus: update-script-behavior.sh about 60 s, install-suite-behavior.sh about 40 s, one Go package about 35 s. Those are the first three files the 30-second budget would flag.

What to change in the batch

  1. Land the Go cuts first, in parallel. Drop depends_on: [REQ-539] from REQ-540 and depends_on: [REQ-541] from REQ-542; neither edits the other's files. REQ-538 and REQ-540 are 90% of the gate's seconds and can be built by two builders at once. REQ-539 keeps its place behind 538 only because both touch the aggregate runner.
  2. Make the 30-second budget a program, not a sentence. Each test file or Go package in the fast tier finishes under 30 s wall. The gate measures every file it runs, prints the durations, appends them to a log next to the calibration log, and fails the fast tier when a file exceeds the budget. The heavy tier has no budget. This is the same shape as REQ-519's line-count ratchet that REQ-539 already lands, so it belongs in REQ-539's acceptance criteria for shell files and REQ-540's for Go packages. The maintainer's instruction was to keep measuring because these suites grow back; a log that the board can read as a verify finding keeps that visible without anyone remembering to check.
  3. Rewrite REQ-541 for per-REQ status. After a green fast gate, a REQ whose diff matches --heavy-surfaces is archived as usual and its record moves to pending-heavy-testing; the loop continues. The status has to be recognized in three places at once: the do-work-cli status vocabulary and selection (skip it, never treat it as malformed), the board's parser and Needs Input classification (the prime asks for parser and board in lock-step), and Step 6.5 in work.md. The maintainer runs --heavy when they choose and clears the status by hand. The standing sweep REQ, its Open Question, and the "one file appended to" wording go.
  4. Start the runner now, not after REQ-542. The gate runner already exists since 0.266.9. Started by hand today, every claim finds its baseline green and skips one of the two runs, which is about 10 minutes per REQ at the current gate length. REQ-542 only automates the start.
  5. Run one gate on this machine at a time. UR-104 was briefed on a 301 s gate; the REQ-537 record shows 641 s for the same tier hours later, and the Codex session measured the heavy tier at 363 s. The spread is machine load: two checkouts, cloud sessions and a Codex worktree were building and testing at once. Budgets measured under that load will trip falsely. The duration log from item 2 should record the concurrent gate count with each row so an over-budget row can be read correctly.

The queue's shape

Twenty of the 38 pending REQs are ready now; their dependencies are archived. The other 18 sit behind five chains. Most are two or three deep and clear as their head lands. One is different: REQ-502 through REQ-510 is a nine-step serial rewrite of the work loop (the read-only advance command, then selection, gates, archive and finalize moved behind it, then capture templates and work-reference sweeps), with REQ-486 behind it. Every link is a rule change that depends on the previous one, so it is genuinely serial. At the recent median that chain alone is about 11 hours of pipeline time, and it is the queue's critical path however many builders run.

Draining everything else is throughput-bound instead: 28 REQs at 65 minutes is about 30 hours serial, and the ready set can be built in parallel under one orchestrator. Shortening the gate takes roughly a third off both figures. Starting the advance chain early, in its own lane, takes the critical path off the end of the drain.
