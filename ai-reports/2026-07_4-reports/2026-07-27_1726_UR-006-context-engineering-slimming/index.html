The number that went the wrong way

The full orchestrator load — what a do-work run actually pulls in — ended higher than it started. The slimming work drove it down to 23,834 words. Then the lock guard (REQ-018) added 2,542 back.

That is a real trade, not an accounting artifact. The guard prevents a class of silent data loss that has already happened once on this repo. Most of its bulk is the failure-mode statements that stopped it being wrong across two failed review rounds. Trimming it is possible; doing so without reintroducing those bugs is the hard part.

REQ-018 — Concurrent-orchestrator lock guard d4cd322

On 2026-07-01 two sessions ran the work loop on one tree simultaneously. The second committed the first's in-flight build and archived it with a hollow paper trail; it merged by luck. Nothing detected the collision. Step 1 now acquires do-work/orchestrator-lock.json before the checkpoint read, before crash recovery, and before the queue scan.

  • Fresh lock, interactive: three choices — proceed anyway, take over, abort.
  • Fresh lock, non-interactive: refuses and reports the holder. When it cannot confirm a human is reachable, it assumes there isn't one.
  • Heartbeat > 45 min: holder presumed dead — warn, take over, let existing crash recovery self-heal. No manual file deletion, ever.
Receipts — two failed review rounds tier 3

Round one: FAIL. Two defects, both in the option sold as the safe one.

  • "Proceed anyway" rewrote the lock to the arriving session, so the original session hit a session-id mismatch at its next checkpoint and was told to stop — mid-build, with its REQ left claimed and abandoned. The choice advertised as leaving the other session alone was the one that killed it.
  • Its skip-crash-recovery decision lived only in conversational memory. On the second loop iteration — after the skill's own mandatory context wipe — crash recovery would fire on the other session's live file.

Root cause was in the REQ's own requirements: "proceed anyway must rewrite the lock" collided with the mismatch-means-stop rule the same REQ implied. The builder followed the contract faithfully and produced a bug. Fixed with a coexisting_sessions array: one holder, coexisting sessions recorded alongside — which is also the durable, file-backed marker the second defect needed.

Round two: FAIL. Step 8 cleared claimed_req at substep 1, but the file doesn't leave working/ until substep 6. For those four substeps the file sat there looking unclaimed — and another live session's ordinary crash recovery would strip its Implementation Summary and re-queue it. That is the 2026-07-01 incident, reintroduced by its own fix.

Fixed by putting lock bookkeeping adjacent to the physical move at all four sites, each carrying a one-line statement of the failure it prevents. Orchestrator load: 23,834 → 26,376 words.

Loose fix — capture's domain stated as the closed set it is 4b11ea0

Found by a cold-start smoke test of the freshly split capture.md. The field was described with an "e.g." list, reading as a free-text hint. It isn't: anything outside the six values normalizes to general with a warning, so an invented domain silently costs you the crew rules the REQ meant to load.

What the smoke test actually proved tier 3

An agent starting cold from actions/capture.md alone opened the companion for the first time at Step 5 — exactly where the pointer says to — went straight to the named sections without hunting, and produced a UR + REQ pair matching the templates field-for-field with no invented fields. Every step before Step 5 was self-contained.

That is the split working. The domain wording was the only real defect it surfaced.
